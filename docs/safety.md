# Choose a safe project

Read the complete project before obtaining parts or dismantling anything. Match the instructions to the exact object, materials and equipment. These editorial notes help identify constraints; they do not replace training, manufacturer instructions or qualified inspection.

## Repair and stored energy

Do not casually dismantle microwaves, CRT displays, mains appliances, high-voltage supplies, pressurized equipment, gas systems or damaged lithium batteries. Disconnecting power does not necessarily discharge stored energy. Do not puncture, bend or open a battery; stop if it is swollen, damaged or unexpectedly hot and seek appropriate professional service.

Back up computer data and use model-matched service instructions. Keep screws and connectors organized. A teardown may omit reassembly steps or damage parts, so it is not automatically a repair guide. Start on a low-value object; restoring an antique can irreversibly remove historic material or value.

A repair is complete only after appropriate function and safety checks. The dataset distinguishes publisher instructions from editorial suggestions. Bicycle brakes, steering, wheel retention, structural joints, load-bearing products and protective gear have consequences beyond appearance; use qualified help when you cannot verify a safe result.

## Tools, materials and space

- Secure workpieces and control sharp edges, dust and projectiles. Follow power-tool training and the protective measures for the actual operation.
- Forging, welding, machine finishing and glass work need suitable workshop controls. Do not treat them as casual apartment projects.
- Use dedicated non-food dye vessels; check mordant chemistry, heat, ventilation and disposal. Natural does not mean harmless.
- Keep clay dust controlled and arrange appropriate firing access. Air-dry craft clay is not automatically watertight, heatproof or food-safe.
- Do not point improvised viewers, lenses or spectroscopes at the Sun or use them for direct solar viewing.
- Watch movements contain stored spring energy. Use a cheap training movement and learn correct release procedures before disassembly.
- Keep small parts, sharp tools and hot glue away from unsupervised children. A homemade toy needs appropriate materials, finishes and small-parts precautions.
- Restrict cybersecurity exercises to the designated labs or targets you have explicit permission to test.

## Risk labels

`low` means ordinary precautions for the inspected scope. `moderate` means meaningful tool, material or device risks require care. `specialist-only` means training or specialist controls are needed. `varies` means the source library spans different risks; select and assess a particular project. Null means risk has not been assessed. Labels are editorial judgments, not certifications.
